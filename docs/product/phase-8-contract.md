## Tool decision

No session requires or opens an AI or external tool. The **AI Adoption Decision Simulator** is built in-product, with fixed synthetic evidence, choices and consequences supplied below. It is free to use within the portal and needs no additional account, API key, model service, upload or external dependency. The portal's saved fields hold the scenario pack; a separate poster or presentation tool is unnecessary. The book's optional AI stress-test is replaced by the simulator's consequence cards and the student's own counterarguments. No chatbot output is needed for completion.

Downloads provide the same evidence and a branching paper walkthrough if the training centre wants an offline activity; students still enter their own choices and reasoning in the portal. No pre-filled recommendation is a fallback answer. Because no session opens a tool, there is no `lab` activity, tool link or acknowledgement in this chapter. The four standing safety notes remain unchanged and apply separately to every session if an AI tool is introduced in a later contract change. The verbatim optional level-up is ordinary source research, not an instruction to open an AI tool or buy access; the training centre can supply accessible forecast extracts with their original sources.

Deterministic model, computed in the browser from a valid path and the final node's vector; no randomness, fitted weights or overall score:

```text
node = start
for each { nodeId, choiceId } in path:
  require nodeId == node.id and choiceId belongs to node.choices
  edge = node.choices.find(choice => choice.id == choiceId)
  node = nodes.find(candidate => candidate.id == edge.next)
require node.choices is empty before recording a completed run
metrics = zip(metricKeys, node.metrics)
hoursReleased = 10 - humanHours
capacityValueEUR = hoursReleased * 20 - costEUR
manual = 100 - automated - assisted
wrongTotal = wrongA + wrongB
rateA = wrongA / 80 * 100
rateB = wrongB / 20 * 100
gap = abs(rateB - rateA)
```

Metrics describe the same workload of 100 requests in one model week; automatic, assisted and manual counts are disjoint and sum to 100. `humanHours` includes checking, exception handling and training time for that workload. `capacityValueEUR` is a comparison of released task capacity less extra costs, not cash profit, wages actually saved or a forecast of redundancies. Show hours to one decimal, euro to two decimals and rates/gap to two decimals, calculated from unrounded values. Show raw counts with rates; the gap is percentage points, not percent change. Wrong outcomes are service safety/reliability failures in this exercise; the model does not estimate injury severity or establish fairness from one number. Group B means a support route, not a demographic label. Energy is the stated invented index and retention is additional AI storage; zeros do not mean the entire service uses no energy or holds no personal data.

Worked numbers for the key and tests: baseline/no-deployment observation gives `8/100` wrong, A `4/80 = 5.00%`, B `4/20 = 20.00%`, gap `15.00` points and net capacity value `€0.00`. `pilot → support` gives `0.5` hours released, `0.5 × 20 − 40 = −€30.00`, 10 automatic / 10 assisted / 80 manual, `5/100` wrong, A `3/80 = 3.75%`, B `2/20 = 10.00%`, gap `6.25` points, seven retention days and two energy units. `human → resource` gives `2.0` hours released, `2 × 20 − 50 = −€10.00`, `3/100` wrong, A `1.25%`, B `10.00%`, gap `8.75` points. `full → speed` gives `8.0` hours released, `8 × 20 − 60 = €100.00`, `14/100` wrong, A `5.00%`, B `50.00%`, gap `45.00` points, 90 retention days and eight energy units. `none → train` gives `1.0` hour released, `1 × 20 − 30 = −€10.00`, `6/100` wrong, A `3.75%`, B `15.00%`, gap `11.25` points. These illustrate competing priorities, not a numerical answer key for the recommendation.
