# Presentation guide (Chapter 8, hour 8 "Sprint 4 · Demo and reflection")

**3–5 minutes, in this order.** Eight points in five minutes is roughly thirty seconds each, so plan one sentence and one piece of evidence per point.

1. **The problem, and who experiences it** — name the problem in one sentence and say who has it. Not "people", a person you actually spoke to.
2. **The evidence you gathered** — what you asked, how many people, and what they said. Say how small the sample is before anyone else does.
3. **The solution, and why AI is or isn't appropriate** — the option you chose and the ones you rejected, including the non-AI one. "AI added complexity without adding value, so we didn't use it" is a top-level answer here.
4. **A demonstration of the prototype** — say what you would show and in what order. A demo proves it runs; it does not prove it is useful.
5. **What happened during testing** — what your testers did, where they got confused, what failed, and the one thing you did not expect.
6. **One change you made because of evidence** — the change, and the specific observation that caused it. Anyone should be able to trace one to the other.
7. **One important risk, and its safeguard** — one risk that matters, and the safeguard. Then what remains after the safeguard, because something always does.
8. **What's still uncertain, or what you'd test next** — name something genuinely uncertain and the test that would settle it. A project with nothing uncertain has not been tested.

## The myth this is assessed against

"Hide the limitations in the presentation." Actually: naming the limitations is one of the things you're being assessed on. Teams that hide them look like teams that didn't test.
