## Tool decision

Primary: **DuckDuckGo AI Chat**, `https://duck.ai` — free, no account, using its free text chat. Fallback: **Microsoft Copilot** on the web, `https://copilot.microsoft.com`, using chat without signing in. Second fallback in every AI lab: `ai-work-sample-outputs.txt`, with a matching sample for that session. If either tool asks for an account, payment or an unavailable feature, move to the next fallback. No uploads, paid features or account-dependent office integrations are required. Provider checks: [DuckDuckGo's no-account AI description](https://duckduckgo.com/duckduckgo-help-pages/duckai/approach-to-ai) and [Microsoft's free, unsigned-in Copilot description](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/topic/understanding-the-different-microsoft-copilot-experiences-cfff4791-694a-4d90-9c9c-1eb3fb28e842).

The training centre supplies **LibreOffice Calc** for the spreadsheet and **Writer** if the student wants a separate document: free desktop tools, no account, installed before the session (`https://www.libreoffice.org/download/`; [suite description](https://www.libreoffice.org/)). The portal's textfields can hold the final briefing. The AI sample fallback replaces the chatbot interaction, not the student's own spreadsheet cleaning, calculations or final work product.

Safety notice for every session that opens the AI tool (verbatim standing notes, with the tool name filled): "Never type your name, address, school, photos, or anything about another person into an AI tool. Use made-up details if a prompt needs them." · "No accounts. DuckDuckGo AI Chat works without signing in; if a tool asks you to sign in, stop and use the fallback." · "The AI is not a fact source. Confident wording is not confident truth. Anything you will rely on gets checked." · "Nothing typed into this portal is sent to the tool; copy across yourself and paste short extracts back here."

These four notes and the acknowledgement-gated link appear separately in b6s1, b6s2, b6s4 and b6s6. Each uses `tool: { name: "DuckDuckGo AI Chat", url: "https://duck.ai", free: true }`, `privacy` equal to the four notes, and `fallback: { title: "Tool blocked?", steps: [try Microsoft Copilot at copilot.microsoft.com without signing in; otherwise open this session's sample in ai-work-sample-outputs.txt; record the same evidence and identify the sample route] }`. b6s3 closes the AI tool; b6s5 reviews saved output independently; b6s7 and b6s8 do not open a tool.

Every session keeps `intro`, `study{title, body[3], example, keywords}`, `reflection`. Study text is written for a 15–16 year old from the book's own explanations; examples use a different small task so they do not hand over this budget's answers. The four "Think about it" questions (p.30) are the reflections for b6s3 ("What did you understand today that you couldn't explain before?"), b6s5 ("Which part of this work are you personally accountable for?"), b6s6 ("Which AI suggestion did you reject, and why?") and b6s7 ("Would you be comfortable explaining your AI use to the training centre or an employer?", adapted for the deployment context). b6s8's reflection is "Where in your workflow must a person stop, check or approve before the next step, and why?"

**b6s1 lab.** `steps` (5: choose one safe learning question; predict which conversation will leave evidence of learning; ask "Give me the answer"; start a fresh conversation in the same tool and ask "Teach me through questions", answering before requesting more help; compare the evidence of the student's own thinking). `fields` (4): "The learning question and my prediction…", "The answer-giving prompt and a short extract of what came back…", "The tutoring prompt, a question it asked, and my attempt…", "Which conversation shows more learning, and what I could defend to the training centre…". Downloads: `ai-work-tutor-cards.txt`, sample outputs. Evidence label "Answer-giving and tutoring comparison".

**b6s2 lab.** `steps` (5: choose a concept and write what you currently understand; ask the AI to find out what you already know; ask for an example and questions, with the full explanation held back until you have tried; answer and request feedback; keep the exchange and note any prompt repair when the tutor gives the answer too soon). `fields` (4): "The concept I chose and what I understood before…", "My AI tutor prompt, exactly as sent…", "The conversation: its example and questions, my attempts, and its feedback…", "What I learned so far, what is still unclear, and how I repaired the tutor prompt if needed…". Downloads: tutor cards, sample outputs. The sample route uses the supplied concept and staged questions; students attempt each before reading the next sample turn and label that evidence as a sample interaction. Evidence label "AI tutor prompt and learning note".

**b6s3 textfields.** Close the AI tool and hide the saved conversation first. Fields: "My explanation of the concept from memory, before reopening any notes…", "Compared with my starting point in b6s2: what I can now explain and what I still cannot…", "After my memory attempt: the example or non-AI source I checked, and what needs another attempt…". If there is no improvement, record that honestly and say how the tutor prompt needs to change. A self-paced written explanation replaces the optional partner explanation. Evidence label "Explanation without AI".

**b6s4 lab (data-cleaning workflow).** `title: "Workplace workflow: clean a messy event budget"`, `file: "ai-work-event-budget-raw.csv"`. The ROADMAP brief is a fictional training-centre open day: the events team has a cost export, meeting notes and a request for a short cost briefing. It needs a defensible subtotal, category breakdown and explicit unresolved items before approving spending. The student decides where AI helps; the spreadsheet and source notes determine the numbers. This is a first-class lab with visible steps and assessable evidence, not an instruction to "clean it up" in study text.

`steps` (8, labels fixed): **Preserve** — save the original as `raw_v1` and make a separate working copy; **Profile** — count rows, fields, blanks, exact duplicate surplus rows, conflicting IDs and suspicious values; **Define rules** — record valid categories, units, ranges and duplicate rules from the brief in a data dictionary; **Clean** — make only justified changes in the working copy and log each changed, removed or flagged value; **Validate** — repeat the profile, reconcile every removed or held row, and distinguish resolved issues from those still flagged; **Analyse** — use spreadsheet formulas for line costs, an eligible subtotal, category totals and a mean eligible line cost, stating the denominator and exclusions; **AI assist** — copy only the synthetic, validated figures and limitations into the tool, ask for critique or wording and keep the prompt and short output; **Human review** — continue in b6s5, check every numerical claim independently, remove unsupported wording and sign off a chart or concise summary. The review calls this last step "Human verify"; the portal uses the book's "Human review" label. The first seven steps occupy this session; the eighth has the book's separate 25-minute session.

`fields` (7): "Preserve: my raw_v1 and working-copy filenames, and how I kept the original untouched…", "Profile: my before counts, with the suspicious rows or fields…", "Define rules: my data dictionary, including categories, units, ranges and what counts as a duplicate…", "Clean: my Data Cleaning Log (Row / field | Original | Issue | Action | Reason | Verified by)…", "Validate: my after counts, changes reconciled, and unresolved flags…", "Analyse: my formulas, results, included row count and exclusions…", "AI assist: my prompt, the figures and limits I supplied, and its short suggested interpretation…". The log records every intervention, including flag-only decisions; `Verified by` names a source, rule or record comparison, not a student's personal name. Study text includes the review's integrity rule verbatim: "Never silently change the data. If a change cannot be justified, flag the value rather than inventing a correction."

Downloads: raw CSV, `ai-work-workplace-brief.txt`, `ai-work-data-dictionary.csv`, `ai-work-cleaning-log.csv`, `ai-work-validation-checklist.csv`, sample outputs. Preserve the workbook locally with raw, working, profile, rules, cleaning log, validation and analysis sheets; paste the evidence into the seven fields so it is reviewable in the portal. The existing read-only CSV viewer is the raw reference, not a spreadsheet editor. No browser-side cleaning model, automatic corrections or file-upload service is introduced. Evidence label "Data-cleaning workflow and spreadsheet analysis".

**b6s5 textfields.** Fields: "My filled human-review checklist: each numerical or factual claim | spreadsheet cell/formula or brief source | supported / uncertain / wrong | what I changed…", "AI claims I removed or qualified, and the evidence for those decisions…", "My final concise cost summary, or chart with its data and caption: verified figures, included count and unresolved limitations…", "My human sign-off: what I checked myself, what still needs a source, and who must approve before spending…". Evidence label "Filled human-review checklist and final work product".

**b6s6 lab.** `steps` (5: read the team's request in the brief; write your own short professional email or briefing from b6s5's checked summary; ask the AI to edit for clarity and tone while preserving meaning, figures and caveats; compare every suggestion with your draft and evidence, accepting or rejecting it with a reason; save your final version with a short AI-use disclosure). `fields` (4): "My own draft before AI editing…", "My editing prompt and the suggestions it returned…", "What I changed or rejected, and why, checked against my evidence…", "My final email or briefing, including what AI contributed and what I checked…".
